## Specific direction carried into Phase 2

1. Keep a continuous fictional Launch Planning example across call, transcript and summary. It must be identified as illustrative.
2. Establish hierarchy through live conversation versus readable record, rather than equal feature tiles.
3. Use the actual interface's dark surfaces as product evidence within a light reading environment; final colors require measurement.
4. Compare an editorial serif/sans option against a single expressive sans system using the same real copy. Select based on rendered legibility and character, not font reputation.
5. Preserve explicit context labels for illustrative/staged visuals even where a researched skill bans captions.
6. Prefer an intentional user-controlled product demonstration over a long autoplay/scroll sequence; determine final interaction after layout study.
7. Close with useful practical answers that the code supports; omit unsupported policies and pricing.

## Remaining visual research questions

For the references actually used to justify art direction, inspect: heading wrap and type roles, reading measure, grid alignment, screenshot scale/cropping, dense/open rhythm, touch behavior and motion. Record screenshots or precise visual observations in the Phase 2 update. Do not promote content research to evidence of visual excellence or conversion performance.

Phase 2 update: the owner requested deferring expensive browser automation. The direction therefore follows the product, source research and explicit design judgment, with an offline typography specimen and calculated palette contrast. No external reference was visually measured, and no rendered comparison result is claimed. Later targeted visual review may refine implementation without reopening the product narrative.
